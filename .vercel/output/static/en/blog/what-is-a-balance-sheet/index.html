What a balance sheet actually tells you

18 August 2026

Most self-employed people only look at their books when a tax return is due. A balance sheet is the tool that changes that: a snapshot of your business at a specific moment, not just a tally of what you’ve invoiced.

What it shows

A balance sheet has three parts:

  • Assets: everything your business has — cash in the bank, invoices you’re owed, equipment, premises if you own them.
  • Liabilities: everything your business owes — loans, unpaid supplier invoices, taxes you haven’t settled yet.
  • Equity: the difference between the two. In practice, this is what your business is worth right now.

Why look at this and not just your profit and loss

Your profit and loss statement tells you whether you made or lost money over a period. A balance sheet tells you something different and complementary: whether your business is solvent, whether you’re too dependent on clients paying on time, or whether debt is piling up faster than you think.

It’s common to discover things through a balance sheet that a P&L never shows — for example, that most of your “profit” is sitting in unpaid invoices, and you actually don’t have enough cash to cover this month’s own bills.

How often to check it

Quarterly is a good rhythm for a freelancer or a small company: enough time for a trend to become visible, not so long that you react too late. This is exactly the difference between just filing your taxes and getting a balance sheet you can actually read and use to decide something — which is how we work.
