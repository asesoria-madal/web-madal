Setting up an SL isn't as quick as registering as self-employed, but it also isn't the long, expensive process many people imagine.

## Share capital isn't the barrier it used to be

Since the reform of Spain's law on business creation and growth, an SL can be set up with a minimum share capital of just €1, instead of the €3,000 previously required. The condition: as long as the share capital stays below €3,000, the company must put 20% of each year's profit into a legal reserve until it reaches that figure, and the shareholders are jointly and severally liable for the gap between the capital they put in and €3,000 if the company is wound up with debts. In practice, it's still reasonable to contribute more than €1 if the business needs real solvency from day one.

## The steps involved

1. **Negative company name certificate**, from the Central Companies Registry — confirms your chosen company name isn't already taken.
2. **Opening a bank account** in the name of the company being formed, to deposit the share capital (with low capital amounts, some notaries accept a declaration of liability instead of a bank certificate).
3. **Public deed before a notary**, where the company's bylaws are approved.
4. **Getting a provisional tax ID** and registering for tax purposes with the Tax Agency (form 036).
5. **Registration with the Companies Registry** for your province, which is what gives the company full legal personality.

## How long it takes

Using standardised template bylaws through Spain's online fast-track system (CIRCE), the whole process can be resolved in a matter of days. With bespoke bylaws, drafted for the company's specific needs, the usual timeframe is several weeks, since each step depends on the previous one and on the notary and registry involved.
