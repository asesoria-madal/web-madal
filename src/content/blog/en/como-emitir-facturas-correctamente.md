A formatting error on an invoice isn't just cosmetic — it can invalidate it as a tax document, both for you and for whoever is paying it.

## The mandatory details

- A sequential invoice number, with no gaps or duplicates within the same series.
- The date of issue.
- Your full details and tax ID, and your client's — name or company name, tax ID, and address if it's a business.
- A clear description of the service or product.
- The taxable base, the VAT rate applied and the resulting amount, and, where it applies, the income tax withholding.
- The total amount.

## Common mistakes

- Duplicate numbering, or several series with no clear logic distinguishing them.
- Leaving out the income tax withholding when it applies: many self-employed people only think about VAT and forget that, when invoicing another professional or business, a withholding almost always needs to be applied too.
- Using a simplified invoice (what used to be a "ticket") when the client actually needs a full invoice to deduct the expense.

## When to apply an income tax withholding

If you invoice professional services to another business or self-employed person, you generally need to include an income tax withholding on the invoice — at a reduced rate during your first years registered as self-employed, if that applies to you. This is one of the most common mistakes when starting out, because it isn't intuitive that the withholding goes on your own income invoice.
