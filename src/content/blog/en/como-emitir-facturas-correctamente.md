An invoice with a formal error is not just a cosmetic detail: it can stop being valid as a tax record, both for you and for whoever is paying you.

## The mandatory details

- A sequential invoice number, with no gaps or duplicates within the same series.
- Date of issue.
- Your full details and NIF, and your client's: name or company name, NIF/CIF, and address if it's a company.
- A clear description of the transaction.
- Taxable base, the VAT rate applied and the resulting amount, and, where it applies, the IRPF withholding.
- Total amount.

## Common mistakes

- Duplicate numbering, or several series with no clear criteria telling them apart.
- Leaving out the IRPF withholding when it applies: many self-employed workers only think about VAT and forget that, when invoicing another professional or company, a withholding almost always has to be applied too.
- Using a simplified invoice (what used to be a "ticket" receipt) when the client actually needs a full invoice to deduct the expense.

## When to apply an IRPF withholding

If you invoice professional services to another company or self-employed worker, as a general rule you must include an IRPF withholding on the invoice, at a reduced rate for the first years after registering as self-employed, if that applies to you. It's one of the most common mistakes when starting out, because it isn't intuitive that the withholding goes on your own income invoice.
