These are the 2027 dates that matter most to freelancers and small businesses, showing the last day of each deadline.

**This is a provisional calendar.** The Tax Agency normally publishes its own in December. We have worked out these dates using the rules that repeat every year, moving deadlines that end on a weekend to the next working day. We'll review it when the official one comes out. It may vary due to local holidays or last-minute changes.

## The four dates you can't miss

If you're a freelancer or have a small SL, your year revolves around these quarterly deadlines:

| Quarter | Withholding (111, 115, 123) | VAT and advance payments (303, 130, 131, 349) |
|---|---|---|
| Fourth of 2026 | **20 January** | **1 February** |
| First of 2027 | 20 April | 20 April |
| Second of 2027 | 20 July | 20 July |
| Third of 2027 | 20 October | 20 October |

**Watch out for January:** it's the only quarter when withholding comes before VAT. In 2027, 30 and 31 January also fall on a weekend, so the VAT and income tax deadline moves to 1 February.

This calendar is for those who file quarterly. If you file monthly, like large companies or those on the monthly refund register, your deadlines are different.

## If you pay by direct debit, you have less time

To pay by direct debit, the deadline ends a few days earlier. In 2026, for example, form 303 could be set up for direct debit until 15 April, July and October, compared with the filing deadline on the 20th. 2027 will be similar, but the exact dates, especially in January, need confirming in the official calendar.

## What happens if you miss a deadline?

Filing late without a request from the Tax Agency carries a surcharge, and if the Tax Agency detects it first, there may be a penalty. The sooner you put it right, the better. If you can see you won't make it, write to us before the deadline.

## How to avoid surprises

- **Upload your invoices in good time.** Leaving it to the last few days is what causes the most errors.
- **Set the money aside during the quarter.** The VAT you charge isn't yours, so it's best to put it to one side.
- **Check which forms apply to you.** Not everyone files them all. We explain this in [forms for freelancers](/en/blog/interpretar-modelos-fiscales-aut) and [forms for SLs](/en/blog/interpretar-modelos-fiscales-sl).
