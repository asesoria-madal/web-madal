If you're self-employed in Spain, the Tax Agency makes you use one of two systems to work out your income tax on business activity. The difference isn't just paperwork — it changes how much you actually pay, depending on how your business runs.

## Direct estimation (estimación directa)

You're taxed on your real profit: what you invoice minus the expenses you can justify (rent, utilities, materials, your social security contributions, and so on). The more deductible expenses you have and can prove, the less you pay. This is the default system unless you both qualify for and choose the flat-rate regime instead.

It suits you if your activity carries real costs you can back up with invoices.

## Flat-rate regime (módulos / estimación objetiva)

Here your tax isn't based on what you actually earned. It's based on fixed indicators the Tax Agency sets for your type of activity: square metres of your premises, electrical power, number of employees, and so on. You pay a roughly fixed amount regardless of whether that quarter was busy or slow.

Only certain activities qualify, mostly retail, hospitality and some trades, and there are turnover limits to be eligible at all.

It can work in your favour if your business has few deductible expenses but invoices consistently, since you often end up paying less than you would calculating real profit.

## How to decide

There's no single right answer. It depends on your specific activity, your real expenses, and how much you invoice. The turnover limits and the list of eligible activities for the flat-rate regime change with each year's regulations, so it's worth confirming your actual situation before committing.
