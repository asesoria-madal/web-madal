## You can be profitable and run out of money at the same time

It's one of the most common shocks in fast-growing businesses: the profit and loss account says you're making money, but in a given month you can't cover payroll or suppliers. It's not a contradiction - it's the difference between **accounting profit** and **actual cash**.

## Why these two things diverge

- You invoice a sale today, but the client pays you in 30, 60 or even 90 days - profit is recorded when you invoice, the money arrives much later.
- You pay your suppliers before you collect from your own clients, if your payment terms are shorter than your collection terms.
- The VAT you charge isn't yours: you collect it, but you have to pay it to the tax office (usually every quarter) - if you mistake it for available cash, you may come up short when the quarterly return is due.
- There are large one-off expenses (an investment, an annual insurance payment) that aren't spread month by month in your cash, even though their accounting effect is.

## What a cash flow forecast is, in practice

It's simply a month-by-month table (or even week-by-week if the business is tight on cash) with:
- **Expected cash inflows**: payments from clients based on their real payment terms, not the invoice date.
- **Expected cash outflows**: payroll, suppliers, rent, social security contributions, tax payments (with their real due dates, not rough estimates).
- The running balance month by month, to see at what point in the year your available cash might drop to a dangerous level.

You don't need a complex system to get started: a spreadsheet with these three columns already gives you visibility that most self-employed workers and small businesses don't have.

## The warning sign to watch ahead of time

What matters isn't today's balance, but the lowest projected balance in the coming months. If the forecast shows that in a given month the balance will be very tight or negative, you have time to react (negotiate terms with a supplier, bring forward a payment, apply for a credit line) if you spot it weeks in advance - you don't have that room if you find out on the day itself.
