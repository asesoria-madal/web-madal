When a company (SL) needs a car, the question is almost always the same: buy it, finance it with a lease, or take it on long-term rental (*renting*). Before comparing the three, there's one question that matters more than any other: will the car have personal use?

## Before choosing: personal use or 100% business use?

If the car belongs to a sales rep, is a works van, or a fleet vehicle used only for work, it's 100% business-use and everything it costs (the payment, depreciation, fuel, insurance, maintenance) is deductible for corporate income tax.

If the owner or director also uses it for personal life (typical of a "management car"), the personal-use portion is charged as a **benefit in kind** on the payroll of whoever uses it, with its own income-tax cost and withholding. Handled that way, that portion is still deductible for the company, like any staff remuneration; what can't be deducted is personal use that goes undeclared. The benefit is valued at 20% per year of the acquisition cost (taxes included) if the company owns the car, or 20% of the new vehicle's market value if it's on rental or lease, with a reduction of between 15% and 30% for energy-efficient vehicles (the maximum for battery-electric and plug-in hybrids). In this scenario, the tax saving from "putting it in the company's name" is smaller than it looks — the user ends up paying it through income tax — and the choice between buying, renting or leasing becomes secondary.

## Buying: slow depreciation, cash tied up

The company buys the car and depreciates it under the official tables (passenger cars, up to 16% a year). The deduction is spread over many years and the outlay is full from the start, or it creates debt. It makes sense if there's spare cash and the car will be used for many years.

## Leasing: accelerated depreciation if you keep it

A lease is financing with a purchase option: on the books you capitalise the vehicle and depreciate it, but the special regime in article 106 lets you deduct the cost-recovery portion up to **double** the table rate (triple for small companies). In practice, you bring the deduction forward into the early years and reduce your taxable base sooner. It's worth it if the company wants to keep the car and has borrowing capacity.

## Long-term rental (renting): a flat expense with no admin

With renting you pay a monthly fee that includes the rental plus services (insurance, maintenance, roadworthiness test, tyres). You capitalise nothing: the full fee is deductible for the business-use portion. No upfront outlay, predictable cost and zero vehicle-admin paperwork. It usually works out slightly more expensive in total cost, but you're paying for predictability and for not spending time on it. It fits well if you renew the car every 3 or 4 years.

## VAT is the same for all three

Whether you buy, lease or rent, the input VAT on the vehicle (and on its fuel and maintenance) is deducted at **50%** by legal presumption, unless you prove greater business use or you're on the 100% list (taxi, driving school, sales reps, transport, security). We explain it in detail in [how much you can deduct for your car](/en/blog/deducir-coche-autonomo). What changes is *when* you bear that VAT: all at once on a purchase, spread out with a lease, month by month with renting.

## How to decide in your case

With personal use, the first step is to work out the benefit in kind and see what real saving is left. Without personal use, the choice comes down to your cash position, how many years you'll keep the car, and whether you want the asset on your balance sheet: renting to forget about it, leasing to bring the deduction forward while keeping it, buying if you have cash to spare.
