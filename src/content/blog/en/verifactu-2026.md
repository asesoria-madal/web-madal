Search "Verifactu" right now and half the results will give you a date that's no longer true. That's not anyone being careless: the rule has been postponed twice, and a lot of the guides out there haven't caught up. Here's the corrected version.

## What Verifactu is

It's the certified electronic invoicing system pushed by Spain's Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria), part of the obligations set out in the anti-fraud law (Ley 11/2021), developed through Royal Decree 1007/2023. The core idea: every invoice issued through billing software has to be recorded in a way that can't be altered or deleted without leaving a trace — with a digital fingerprint, a timestamp and, in many cases, a QR code you can use to verify it.

The stated goal is to end the practice of keeping one set of books for the tax office and a different, real one on the side.

## The correct date, as of today

Royal Decree-Law 15/2025, of 2 December, pushed the timeline back for the second time. The dates currently in force are:

- **1 January 2027**, for companies and corporations.
- **1 July 2027**, for self-employed workers and other obligated parties.

Before that postponement, the date being quoted everywhere was July 2026 — and that's still what shows up in plenty of guides that haven't been updated in months. If you've seen that date somewhere, you didn't imagine it: it just went stale in December 2025.

## Who this actually affects

Only people who issue invoices through billing software (what the regulation calls a SIF). If you still invoice by hand or on paper, this specific obligation doesn't apply to you — though at this point, that's a rare way to run a business.

## What to do now, without rushing but without ignoring it either

With the deadline sitting in July 2027, there's still room to breathe. But the real change isn't the date, it's the software you invoice with — so it makes more sense to check now whether yours is already certified than to leave it for the last quarter before the deadline.
