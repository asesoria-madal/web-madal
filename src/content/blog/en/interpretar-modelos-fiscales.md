Every quarter we send you a copy of the forms we've filed, and it's common for them to raise questions: what each one is, and why the amount is what it is. Here's the quick reference.

## Model 130 — the income tax instalment

This is your quarterly advance payment towards income tax on your business profit. It's calculated by accumulating income and expenses since January, applying 20%, and subtracting what you've already paid in earlier quarters. We walk through an example in [how quarterly income tax is calculated](/en/blog/calculo-irpf-trimestral).

## Model 303 — quarterly VAT

This compares the VAT you've charged on your issued invoices against the VAT you've paid on your expenses and purchases. If what you charged is higher, you pay the difference; if it's the other way round, it carries forward to be offset in later quarters, or in the last quarter of the year you can request a refund.

## Model 390 — the annual VAT summary

This is informational — nothing is paid separately when filing it. It gathers and reconciles the year's four quarterly model 303 filings, and is filed in January of the following year.

## Model 100 — your annual tax return

This is the final adjustment of your yearly income tax, and it isn't limited to your business activity: it brings in employment income if you have any, your personal and family allowance, and deductions that don't appear in the quarterly calculation. This is where you see whether what you paid throughout the year via model 130 matches what was actually owed — it can result in a refund, or in paying more.

## Where the numbers come from

All of these forms are built from your income and expense ledger, which in turn comes from the invoices you issue and receive. That's why we insist on getting all of your invoices, not just the ones that seem relevant to you — any expense without an invoice simply can't go into the calculation.

If a particular quarter's figure surprises you, ask us directly about that form — we can always break down where the number comes from.
