One of the first numbers anyone considers before registering as self-employed (autónomo) in Spain is how much they'll pay each month to Social Security. Short answer: almost nobody pays the full fee from day one. There's a flat-rate discount first, then a tiered system.

## What the flat rate is and how long it lasts

It's a reduced fee for anyone registering as self-employed for the first time, or who hasn't been registered under RETA in the previous two years. In 2026 the base rate is around €80/month, which with the MEI (Intergenerational Equity Mechanism, an extra 0.9%) rises to roughly €88.64-88.72/month in practice.

It lasts 12 months. If your projected net income stays below the minimum wage, it can be extended to 24.

## Requirements to apply

- Not having been registered as self-employed in the previous 2 years (3 if you already used the flat rate before).
- No outstanding debts with Social Security.
- You request it as part of the registration itself (form TA.0521): it's not retroactive — if you don't ask for it when registering, you lose it.

## What happens once it ends

After the 12 (or 24) months, you move into the tiered system: you pay based on the net income you expect to earn that year, not one fixed fee for everyone. In 2026 there are 15 tiers, ranging from just over €200/month for low income projections up to around €590/month for those expecting more than €6,000/month in net income.

You choose your tier when registering (or when the flat rate ends) based on what you estimate you'll earn, and it can be adjusted during the year if your forecast changes significantly from reality. ## Why guessing your tier is a bad idea

A tier set well below what you'll actually earn can lead to a surcharge adjustment the following year; one set too high means overpaying every month for no reason.
