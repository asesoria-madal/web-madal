If you're self-employed in Spain under direct estimation, you file model 130 every quarter — an advance payment towards your annual income tax. Most people pay it without understanding where the figure comes from, which makes it impossible to plan for.

## What model 130 actually calculates

You pay 20% of your accumulated net result for the year — all your income from 1 January to the end of the quarter, minus all deductible expenses in that same period — minus whatever you've already paid in earlier quarters of that same year, and minus any withholdings other clients have already applied on your behalf.

## A simple example

If in the first quarter you invoice €9,000 with €2,000 in deductible expenses, your profit is €7,000. 20% of that is €1,400, and since it's the first quarter of the year, that's the amount due.

In the second quarter, the calculation doesn't start from zero — it accumulates. If by June you've invoiced €18,000 in total with €4,000 in accumulated expenses, your accumulated profit is €14,000. 20% of that is €2,800, minus the €1,400 already paid in the first quarter. The result: you pay another €1,400 in the second quarter.

## When you don't need to file it

This instalment payment only applies if you're taxed under direct estimation. On top of that, if more than 70% of your business income last year already had income tax withheld at source (typical if you mostly invoice other businesses or professionals), you're exempt from filing model 130 — the Tax Agency has already been collecting through those withholdings.

## Why it doesn't match "what you think you'll owe"

Model 130 is an advance payment, not the final calculation of your income tax. The real adjustment happens the following year in your annual tax return, which brings in your personal allowance, other income if you have any, and deductions that don't show up in the quarterly calculation. That can result in a refund, or in owing more.

## How to estimate it yourself

If you keep your income and expense records up to date, you can apply 20% to the year's accumulated figure and subtract what you've already paid in earlier quarters to get an orientative number ahead of the filing date.
