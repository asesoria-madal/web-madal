A foreigner can set up an SL (a Spanish limited company) even without living in Spain, and can be a partner, a director or both. The steps are the same as for any SL (name, share capital, deed and registration), with a few extra requirements: identifying yourself with an NIE, preparing foreign documents properly and, once the company exists, filing the foreign investment.

## What do I need to set up an SL as a foreigner?

- An **NIE** for every partner and every director who is an individual. It is the number the tax office, the notary and the Companies Registry use to identify a foreigner in Spain.
- A valid **passport or ID document**.
- The **name certificate** from the Central Companies Registry, as for any SL. See [how to choose the name](/en/blog/nombre-sl-denominacion-social).
- **Proof of the share capital**, usually a certificate from the Spanish bank where it is paid in. See [minimum capital for an SL](/en/blog/capital-minimo-sl).
- If the partner is a **foreign company**: its Spanish tax ID, documents proving it exists and who represents it, and the signatory’s power of attorney.

## How do I get an NIE?

You apply at an immigration office or police station in Spain, or at the Spanish consulate in your country. You need to give a reason: setting up a company is one. It is not the same as residency: the NIE only identifies you; it does not allow you to live or work in Spain.

## Can I sign without travelling to Spain?

Yes, in two ways:

1. **At the Spanish consulate** in your country, which acts as a notary.
2. **With a power of attorney** granted in your country to someone who signs in Spain. It must carry the **Hague apostille** (or legalisation, if your country is not part of the Convention) and, if it is not in Spanish, a **sworn translation**.

The same rule applies to any foreign document you provide: apostille and sworn translation.

## Can it be done through CIRCE?

The fast route (CIRCE, with the [Virtual Wallet](/en/blog/cartera-virtual-circe)) needs at least one partner with a **Spanish digital certificate or Cl@ve**. A foreigner can get the certificate with their NIE, but identity has to be proven in person. If nobody has one, there are two options: still file through CIRCE from an in-person **Business Support Point (PAE)**, which submits the procedure with its own certificate, or set the company up the **traditional way**, directly at a notary. We compare both in [CIRCE or the traditional route](/en/blog/montar-sl-circe-o-tradicional).

## Can the director live outside Spain?

Yes. The law does not require the director to live in Spain. Two things are worth bearing in mind:

- **Where the company is run from.** A company set up in Spain is tax resident here and pays Spanish corporate income tax.
- **What the director is paid.** If they do not live in Spain, their pay is taxed under Non-Resident Income Tax, generally at **24%** (19% if they live in another EU or European Economic Area country), unless a double tax treaty says otherwise. The company withholds the tax.

## Does the foreign investment have to be declared?

Yes. When a non-resident invests in a Spanish company, the investment is filed with the Ministry of Economy’s **Investment Register** using **form D-1A**, usually in the month after incorporation. It is an informational filing: there is nothing to pay and no authorisation is needed, except in specific cases (for example, investments from tax havens, which are declared beforehand, or sensitive sectors).

## And the bank account?

To pay in the capital, the bank will ask for your NIE and passport and, as with any company, information about the business and its **beneficial owner** (anti-money-laundering rules). With non-resident partners, opening the account usually takes longer, so it is a good place to start.

Being a foreigner does not stop you from setting up an SL in Spain: what changes is the paperwork beforehand (NIE, apostilles and translations) and the investment filing afterwards.
