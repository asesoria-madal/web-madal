It's very common to mix up two obligations that aren't the same thing: Verifactu and mandatory B2B e-invoicing. They overlap in timing and both get called "digital invoicing," but they regulate different things.

## Verifactu isn't this

Verifactu requires the software you invoice with to record every invoice in a way that can't be altered without leaving a trace. It's an obligation on the software you use, not on the format you send the invoice to your client in. We cover the actual dates in [Verifactu: what it is, and when it actually affects you](/en/blog/verifactu-2026).

## Mandatory e-invoicing (the "Crea y Crece" law)

This is a different law: Ley 18/2022, on the creation and growth of businesses, requires invoices between businesses and self-employed people to be issued and received in a structured electronic format — not just any PDF, but a file a computer system can read and process without manual intervention — and to be able to report the invoice's status (paid, pending, rejected).

## Why the timeline still causes confusion

The regulation developing this obligation, Real Decreto 238/2026, of 25 March, has already been approved and published. It sets a 12-month deadline for businesses with annual turnover above €8 million, and 24 months for everyone else, including the self-employed. What's still not settled is when that countdown actually starts: it depends on a technical Ministerial Order that, as of now, is still pending final approval. Until that order is published, the exact deadline that applies to you isn't fully fixed. We'll confirm the current date with you as it gets closer to affecting you.

## What you can do now

The most useful thing right now is checking that your invoicing software can generate structured electronic format (Facturae or an equivalent standard) — in practice, it's usually the same technical requirement Verifactu asks for, so checking one puts you on track for the other.
