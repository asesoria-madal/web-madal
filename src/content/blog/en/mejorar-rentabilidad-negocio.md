Filing taxes on time is the minimum. Looked at properly, your accounts also tell you whether the business is actually doing well, or just looks like it because there's cash in the account. That's the difference between an accounting firm that files, and one that also advises.

## Start by separating margin from turnover

Invoicing more doesn't always mean earning more. What matters is margin: income minus the direct cost of generating that income. A business can grow its turnover and lose margin at the same time if costs grow faster.

## Watch fixed costs versus variable ones

Fixed costs stay put even in a slow month — rent, social security contributions, subscriptions, staff. Identifying them and reviewing them regularly tells you the minimum you need to invoice each month just to break even, something many people never actually sit down and calculate.

## Look at the business every month, not just every quarter

The quarterly cadence exists because of tax obligations, not because it's the best rhythm for running a business. Reviewing your profit and loss month by month lets you catch a deviation while it's still small, instead of discovering it three months later.
