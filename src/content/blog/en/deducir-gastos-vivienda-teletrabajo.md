If you work from home, part of your housing costs are deductible. The condition is declaring precisely what portion of your home you use for your business, and applying the right calculation to each type of expense.

## The condition: partial business use

The Tax Agency requires you to declare what percentage of your home is dedicated to your business — usually the square metres of the room or space you use as an office or workshop, over the total size of the home. This is what's called "partial business use," and it's the base percentage the deductions are calculated on.

## What you can deduct, and how it's calculated

- **Rent or mortgage interest, property tax, community fees, and home insurance**: proportional to the percentage of square metres dedicated to the business.
- **Utilities — electricity, water, gas, internet**: here the rules apply a different criterion than for rent: you can deduct 30% of the proportional part tied to the business-use square metres, not 100% of that proportion. It isn't the same calculation as for rent, so it's worth having us adjust it to your specific case.
- **Phone**: deductible if it's a line clearly identifiable as professional — the cleanest option is having a separate line from your personal one.

## What you need so it doesn't get challenged

Declaring the business-use percentage on the relevant tax registration form, keeping all your utility and housing invoices in your name, and making sure the declared percentage is reasonable relative to how the space is actually used.

## Why it's worth calculating properly, not roughly

A percentage that's too high can raise questions from the Tax Agency; one that's too low just leaves deductible money on the table.
