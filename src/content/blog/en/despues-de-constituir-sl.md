Once the deed is signed and registered with the Companies Registry, the company exists. But it is not yet ready to invoice with everything in order. This is what needs to be done in the first few weeks, in order.

## 1. Is the tax ID final?

When the company is set up, the tax office gives it a **provisional tax ID (NIF)**. Once the deed is registered with the Companies Registry, the NIF becomes **definitive**. If the company was set up through CIRCE, this step follows on automatically; otherwise, the registered deed has to be submitted to the tax office. With the definitive NIF you can apply for the **company’s digital certificate**, which you will need for almost everything that follows.

## 2. Tax registration: form 036

The **census declaration (form 036)** tells the tax office what the company does and which taxes it will file:

- The activity and its IAE heading (companies do not pay the business activity tax for their first two years, nor afterwards while turnover stays under €1 million).
- The VAT regime and, if it will trade with other EU countries, registration in the intra-EU operators register (ROI).
- The withholding it will apply: to employees or professionals (form 111) or on the rent of its premises (form 115).

It has to be filed before the activity starts.

## 3. Social Security for the director and employees

- **The director or partner who works in the company and controls it** registers as a **company self-employed worker**, even if unpaid. See [company self-employed vs. the general scheme](/en/blog/autonomo-societario-vs-reta-general).
- **If you are going to hire**, the company registers as an employer with Social Security, gets its contribution account code and registers each employee before they start. See [hiring your first employee](/en/blog/contratar-primer-empleado-autonomo).

## 4. The bank account

If the capital was paid in before signing, the account already exists and the bank only has to make it operational with the registered deed. If not, now is the time to open it. It is the account the company will use to receive and make payments: **don’t mix in personal spending**, as it complicates the accounts and what can be deducted.

## 5. Permits for the premises

If the business operates from premises open to the public, a **licence or prior notice** to the town hall is usually required. It depends on the municipality and the activity. If you work from home or a coworking space, generally not.

## 6. Invoicing and accounting from day one

- Invoices carry the **company name**, its tax ID and sequential numbering. See [how to issue invoices correctly](/en/blog/como-emitir-facturas-correctamente).
- The company keeps **full accounts** under the Spanish General Accounting Plan.
- The **books** (journal, inventory and annual accounts book, and minutes book) are legalised electronically with the Companies Registry no later than **four months after** each financial year ends.

## 7. The tax calendar

| Form | What it is | When |
|---|---|---|
| 303 | VAT | Every quarter (every month if registered for monthly refunds or a large company) |
| 111 and 115 | Withholding, if any | Every quarter (every month in the same cases) |
| 390, 190 and 180 | Annual summaries | January |
| 200 | Corporate income tax | July |
| 202 | Instalment payments | April, October and December, when the last corporate income tax return had tax payable (usually not in the first year). Companies with turnover above €6 million always file it |

The **annual accounts** are also prepared within three months of the year end, approved by the shareholders within six months of the year end and filed with the Companies Registry within a month of approval. You can check which forms apply to you with our tool [which tax returns do I need to file?](/en/herramientas/que-impuestos-presento).

## In short

Setting up the SL is the first step. Before invoicing: definitive tax ID, digital certificate, form 036, the director’s Social Security, a bank account and, if needed, a licence for the premises.
